Privacy: Birdspotter and CBC Scout

Last changed November 27, 2026.

This page covers the two phone apps on this site, Birdspotter and CBC Scout. It does not cover the CBC Circle Manager, the compiler's tool; its Help tab says where its data lives.

Neither app has an account, analytics, or a server of my own. I don't receive anything the apps send. What leaves your phone goes straight to the services below.

What leaves your phone

eBird, at api.ebird.org

eBird is run by the Cornell Lab of Ornithology. When the Sightings or Hotspots list needs new data, the app sends eBird:

  • your eBird API key
  • a latitude and longitude, not rounded
  • a search distance
  • for sightings, how many days back to look

In Birdspotter, the place is where you are. It moves only once you are half the search radius away from the last place searched. After you tap Look here, the place is the middle of the map instead, until you tap Near me. The search then moves back to you once you are half the search radius from that place. Birdspotter does not open on that place again, but the saved eBird results below hold it, as they hold every place searched. In CBC Scout with an area loaded, the place is the center of the count circle, not your location. With no area loaded, it is where you are.

OpenFreeMap, at tiles.openfreemap.org

While the map is on screen, the app asks OpenFreeMap for the map style, tiles, fonts, and icons for the part of the map you are looking at. With location on, the map follows you, so the tiles requested show roughly where you are. In CBC Scout, Save street map downloads the map style, icons, fonts, and every tile over a rectangle around the active area's outline.

GitHub Pages, at lizstanley.com

GitHub hosts this site. Opening an app, or the app checking for an update, asks GitHub for the app's own files.

Your IP address

Every request above shows your phone's IP address to the service that answers it.

Only when you tap

  • Directions opens Apple Maps (maps.apple.com) on iPhone and iPad, or Google Maps (www.google.com) everywhere else, with the place's latitude and longitude as the destination.
  • Checklist links, the eBird key request page, and the eBird.org link under the lists open eBird (ebird.org).
  • More about me in About opens my site, lizstanley.com.
  • The map's credits, behind the small i on the map, link to MapLibre (maplibre.org), OpenFreeMap (openfreemap.org), OpenMapTiles (www.openmaptiles.org), and OpenStreetMap (www.openstreetmap.org).
  • In CBC Scout, a leader's phone number or email opens your phone or mail app.
  • In CBC Scout, Share totals hands your party's totals to the phone's share sheet, or copies them. They go only where you send them.

What stays on your phone

The apps keep these in your browser's storage for this site. That storage belongs to lizstanley.com, so every page on lizstanley.com shares it, the apps and the circle manager included. On iPhone and iPad, an app added to the Home Screen keeps its own storage, apart from Safari and the other apps.

  • your eBird key. Saving it in one app saves it for the others in the same browser.
  • saved eBird results. The app shows a saved hotspot list for the rest of the day, and saved sightings for 6 hours at most. Each is saved under its request address, which holds the place searched. Older copies, with their places, stay on the phone until the app next saves eBird data. That removes yesterday's copies once they are 6 hours old, and every copy from earlier days.
  • each app's settings, and whether location is on
  • in CBC Scout, the saved areas and which one is active
  • in CBC Scout, the count session, with its route: the location points recorded while counting
  • each app's own files, so it opens with no signal
  • in CBC Scout, the street maps you save

A saved area holds the area outline, the name, phone, and email of the area's leaders, and your compiler's note for counters. That data comes in the part of the area link after the #, which a browser never sends to a server. CBC Scout removes it from the address bar once it has read it.

CBC Scout's Scan QR code reads camera frames on the phone. No image leaves the phone. Paste from clipboard reads the clipboard only when you tap it.

Remove area takes an area and its street map off the phone, and Remove street map takes only the map. Clearing this site's data in your browser's settings removes everything above from that browser. It also removes the circle manager's saved circles in that browser, so export a backup first if you use it there.
